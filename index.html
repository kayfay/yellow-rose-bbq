Day 1: Grinding, Spicing & Curing

Step 1

Ice down all grinder parts, meat tubs, and water vessels in a large tub for 30 minutes before cutting.

Step 2

Blend recipe weights of lean beef, pork, and hard fat cuts uniformly.

Step 3

Run a rapid coarse grind using a 3/8-inch plate directly into the iced bus tub.

Step 4

Evenly distribute Pink Curing Salt and dry spices across grind.

Step 5

Dry mix with hands for 2 minutes to completely integrate the cure into the meat.

Step 6

Cover tightly and store in walk-in for mandatory overnight cure window.

Day 2: Mixing, Stuffing & Linking

Step 7

Submerge stuffer parts in ice bath for 30 minutes.

Step 8

Prep custom add-in ingredients per selected recipe.

Step 9

Whisk ice water binder mixture with powdered milk and pepper.

Step 10

Pour binder over cured meat and execute heavy manual mixing for 3 mins.

Step 11

Incorporate remaining ice water, cheese cubes, and add-ins.

Step 12

Knead aggressively for 3-5 minutes until meat paste turns sticky.

Step 13

Flush natural hog casings, slide onto horn, and pack cylinder tightly.

Step 14

Extrude onto wet table, pinch at 6-inch intervals, and twist links.

Step 15

Hang links on racks in walk-in for 2+ hours to form pellicle before smoking.

🌧️ Weather Impact on Sales

Step-by-Step Reasoning: How Weather Affects Sales

  • Precipitation Impact (+$97.37/mm): Blending Open-Meteo Jacksonville rainfall data with daily POS transactions reveals that moderate rain shifts ordering behavior to higher-ticket family bundles for at-home dining.
  • Extreme Heat (>90°F): Outdoor patio dining drops significantly during Florida heat waves. Dine-in volume concentrates heavily at the indoor air-conditioned tables, and overflow customers shift toward quick takeout or curbside pickup rather than sitting outside.
  • Operational Choice - Weather Adjustments: Since we avoid margin-killing third-party delivery, leverage heavy rain days by promoting "Rainy Day Vacuum-Sealed Freezer Packs." On days when rainfall exceeds 10mm, reduce walk-in hot brisket prep by 15%, but immediately vacuum-seal the surplus and promote it on social media for customers to stock their freezers. During extreme heat, shift front-of-house staff to expedite curbside pickup to keep customers in their air-conditioned cars.

💡 Executive Insights Summary

1. Sell-Out / Stock Depletion Trajectory
• The "Taco Rush": Crispy Quesa Taco Brisket is a volume monster. Trajectory is flat until 2:00 PM, spikes violently, and climbs through 9:00 PM (a heavy dinner item).
• Ribs are for Dinner: Full Rack Pork Spare Ribs register almost no sales before 4:00 PM, selling entirely during the dinner rush.
• No Early Sell-Outs: Crucially, none of the curves go completely flat before 9:00 PM. This means the restaurant is successfully prepping enough to meet demand through closing time without leaving money on the table.

2. Cannibalization (Dino Ribs vs. Pork Ribs)
Offering Beef Dino Ribs as a special definitively cannibalizes normal Pork Spare Rib sales, dropping average daily racks sold from 6.2 to 3.9 (-37%). Prep targets for pork should be intentionally reduced on Dino Rib days to prevent waste.

3. Order Type Segmentation
• Dine-in (~$44 avg ticket): Primarily individuals or couples buying single plates and sandwiches.
• In-Store Pickup (~$62) & To-Go (~$50): Higher tickets driven by "Family Meals" (buying meat by the pound to feed a household).

4. Weather & Event Interactions
While rain kills walk-in traffic (dropping revenue from ~$4.9k to ~$3.4k), Game Days trigger massive spikes up to ~$17.3k. Even if it rains on a Game Day, revenue stays incredibly high (~$13.8k) as customers simply pivot to To-Go orders for watch parties.

5. The "Payday" Myth
There is virtually no difference in ticket size between Payday weekends ($44.92) and normal days ($45.91). Yellow Rose BBQ acts as a steady, reliable comfort food staple rather than a transient "payday splurge."
