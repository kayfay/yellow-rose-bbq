🔥 TL;DR Why We Stagger Cook Shifts:
  • Cook #1 (Overnight Prep): 5:00 PM – 2:00 AM → Trims, rubs, and loads the initial overnight burn.
  • Cook #2 (Morning Smoke): 2:00 AM – 2:00 PM (sometimes 12 PM) → Manages the long smoke, wraps briskets, pulls ready meat for lunch.
  • Cook #3 (Day/Mid): 8:00 AM – 7:00 PM → Covers lunch rush, dinner prep, and hands off to the evening shift.
  • Why do this? Keeps meat hot & juicy all day and doubles your smoker space so you don't run out!

🧠 Analytical Findings: Tab 1 ARIMA Baseline Model

Step-by-Step Reasoning: Weekly Cycle Baseline

  • The Baseline Trend: By analyzing historical sales data through our Univariate SARIMAX (1,0,1) × (1,1,1,7) model, we've identified the underlying weekly rhythm of our pit operations. The data proves a massive, statistically significant surge occurring every Friday and Saturday.
  • Actionable Insight - Peak Staffing: The forecast expects Saturday revenue to hit our weekly maximum. At a baseline of roughly $4,830 per peak Saturday, standard metrics demand 7 Pit Crew members to maintain the $800/staff service threshold. However, our optimized workflow and staggered shifts allow us to effectively run on a high-efficiency skeleton crew of just 3.
  • Actionable Insight - Meat Procurement: The forecast dictates prepping ~165 lbs of raw brisket and ~85 lbs of pork shoulder for Saturday. Because brisket loses 45% of its weight during the 14-hour smoke, purchasing exactly the projected 165 lbs mathematically ensures we hit our target sell-out time right at closing (8:00 PM - 9:00 PM). Purchasing less risks a premature afternoon sell-out, while purchasing more without external demand triggers risks expensive food waste.

🌧️ Analytical Findings: Tab 2 Weather Impact Regression

Step-by-Step Reasoning: Weather Exogenous Correlation

  • Precipitation Impact (+$97.37/mm): Blending Open-Meteo Jacksonville rainfall data with daily POS transactions reveals that moderate rain shifts ordering behavior to higher-ticket family bundles for at-home dining.
  • Extreme Heat (>90°F): Outdoor patio dining drops significantly during Florida heat waves. Dine-in volume concentrates heavily at the indoor air-conditioned tables, and overflow customers shift toward quick takeout or curbside pickup rather than sitting outside.
  • Operational Choice - Weather Adjustments: Since we avoid margin-killing third-party delivery, leverage heavy rain days by promoting "Rainy Day Vacuum-Sealed Freezer Packs." On days when rainfall exceeds 10mm, reduce walk-in hot brisket prep by 15%, but immediately vacuum-seal the surplus and promote it on social media for customers to stock their freezers. During extreme heat, shift front-of-house staff to expedite curbside pickup to keep customers in their air-conditioned cars.
